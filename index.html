Privacy policy for BulkText

Effective date: 27 October 2026

1. Who we are

BulkText is published by PrivaTools. Questions about this policy: gulfidevwork@gmail.com.

If you email us, we receive your email address and your message, and use them only to answer you.

2. What BulkText does

BulkText is a free, ad-supported SMS app for Android. You set it as your phone's default SMS app. It shows and sends your text messages, and it can send one message you write to many recipients you choose, as separate SMS through your own SIM and phone plan.

BulkText has no account and no server. We, the developer, never receive your messages, contacts or any other content you keep in the app.

3. Data kept on your phone only

BulkText stores the following in its private storage on your phone. None of it is sent to us.

  • Text messages (SMS) and multimedia messages (MMS). As the default SMS app, BulkText reads and writes your messages in Android's SMS storage, the same storage every SMS app uses, and keeps its own copy of message text and status, and of the type and name of each attachment. Pictures, videos, audio, contact cards and files in multimedia messages you receive or send stay in Android's SMS storage. Something you attach to a message, including a voice message you record, is kept in BulkText's private storage until the message is sent or the draft is discarded; leftovers from an interrupted draft are deleted once they are a day old, the next time BulkText starts or you open a conversation. Copies BulkText makes to play a video or to share, open or save an attachment are deleted once they are a day old, the next time BulkText starts or opens an attachment, and at once if BulkText stops being your default SMS app. The encoded copy of each multimedia message BulkText sends or downloads is deleted when the transfer ends; a copy left behind by an interrupted transfer is deleted once it is a day old, the next time BulkText sends or downloads a multimedia message.
  • Scheduled messages: the text, attachments and send time of messages you schedule, kept in BulkText's private storage until they are sent or you delete them.
  • BulkText contacts: the names, phone numbers and custom fields you enter or import, and the consent record you keep for each contact (a consent status and an optional note).
  • Groups, message templates, and broadcasts with each recipient's personalized message and its sent, delivered or failed status.
  • The do-not-message list: numbers that replied with an opt-out word such as STOP, or that you added, with the date and how each was added.
  • Spam and blocked numbers: which conversations you reported as spam, and a copy of the numbers on your phone's block list. As your default SMS app, BulkText reads that list to sort conversations into Spam & blocked and to leave blocked numbers out of broadcasts. It adds a number only when you block it, and removes one only when you unblock it or mark its conversation as not spam while the number is blocked. The list itself belongs to Android, which shares it with your Phone app.
  • A copy of the names and numbers in your phone's contacts, used to show names in the Inbox, in conversations and in notifications, and to let you pick contacts to import. It exists only while BulkText has contacts access.
  • Settings, including your own phone number if you enter it (used to send test messages to yourself) and the SIM you choose for sending.
  • Your ad consent choice, stored on the phone by Google's consent tool (see section 5).

BulkText's data is excluded from Android's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer.

4. Permissions and why BulkText uses them

  • SMS (send, receive and read SMS; receive MMS). These are what an SMS app needs to show, receive and send your messages. Google Play's policy, together with Android's rules for restricted permissions, lets BulkText use them only as your default SMS app, and BulkText asks for them only after you have made it your default SMS app. As your default SMS app, BulkText can also read and change the phone's list of blocked numbers; Android gives that access only to the default SMS app and the Phone app, without a separate permission.
  • Contacts (read only). To show the names saved in your phone's contacts and to let you import contacts you pick. When BulkText is the default SMS app, Android also gives it contacts access (on Android 10 and newer the system dialog lists Contacts), so names from the phone's contacts appear without a separate prompt. Turning contacts off in system settings removes them. To attach a contact card, BulkText reads the one contact you pick; the card contains only that contact's name, phone numbers and email addresses.
  • Phone state (READ_PHONE_STATE). Only to list your phone's SIMs, so you can choose which SIM sends. Nothing it reads leaves your phone.
  • Notifications. To tell you about new messages and to show a broadcast's progress while it sends.
  • Foreground service, start in the background, run at startup, keep awake and alarms. A broadcast sends while BulkText isn't on screen, starts at the time you scheduled or when quiet hours end, and continues after your phone restarts. A message you schedule is sent at the time you chose: on the minute when you allow Alarms & reminders (SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM), otherwise it may be sent late while the phone is idle, usually within about an hour, later with Battery Saver on.
  • Internet and network state. Used by the Google Mobile Ads SDK to load ads and by Google's consent tool. BulkText's own features don't use the internet. The network-state permission is also declared by Android's WorkManager library, which BulkText uses to send broadcasts.
  • Basic phone state (READ_BASIC_PHONE_STATE). Declared by the Google Mobile Ads SDK; it gives read-only access to basic information such as the type of mobile network. Android grants it without asking.
  • Advertising ID. Used by the Google Mobile Ads SDK (see section 5).
  • Microphone (RECORD_AUDIO). While BulkText is your default SMS app, Android lets it use the microphone without asking you. Android gives it microphone access when you make it your default SMS app and, if it already is, when the update that adds voice messages arrives. Only Android 13 and newer list Microphone in the default-app dialog. BulkText uses the microphone only while you record a voice message, after you tap or hold the microphone button; the recording is attached to your draft. You can turn microphone access off in App info → Permissions → Microphone; making BulkText your default SMS app again turns it back on.
  • Location (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION and ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, only while you use BulkText). Only when you tap Location while writing a message, Android asks whether BulkText may use your location, and you can say no. On Android 11 and newer you can allow it only this time. On Android 12 and newer you can also choose approximate instead of precise location; on older versions, allowing location gives BulkText your precise location. BulkText then reads your position once and puts a Google Maps link to it into your message, which you can change or delete before you send it. With approximate location, Android blurs your position to a wider area. BulkText never reads your location in the background and never sends it anywhere except in a message you send. It keeps no copy of its own: only the link in your message, which stays with your messages on this phone. You can change or turn off location access at any time in App info → Permissions → Location; the rest of BulkText works the same without it.

When you make BulkText your default SMS app, Android grants it the permissions of that role that it declares. On Android 13 and newer, Android's dialog lists that role's permission groups, such as Camera, Microphone or Files and media; older versions don't list the microphone, which Android grants all the same (see Microphone above). BulkText declares the microphone, for voice messages, and location, which Android never grants with the SMS role and which BulkText asks for only when you share your location. It doesn't declare camera (it opens your camera app instead), photos and videos, background location or call-log permissions.

5. What leaves your phone

Your messages, through your carrier. The texts you send, including broadcast messages and test messages to yourself, go through your mobile carrier to the recipients you choose, and your carrier's charges apply. Multimedia messages (MMS) you send, with the pictures, videos, voice messages, contact cards and files you attach, go to your carrier's MMS service over your mobile data connection. Pictures and videos you attach in BulkText, except GIFs sent as they are, are resized first, which removes details stored in them such as location. A failed message you retry is sent again as it was stored, so a picture or video attached in another SMS app keeps any such details. Documents and audio files are sent exactly as they are, with their file names and anything stored inside them. Incoming MMS are downloaded from your carrier by Android. Your carrier sends delivery reports where it supports them. If you turn on the automatic reply to opt-outs (off by default), BulkText sends that reply by SMS to a number that has just opted out. Your carrier handles your messages under its own terms and privacy policy. We never receive them. A location you share is a Google Maps link in your message's text; Google may receive it when someone opens the link or when their messaging app shows a preview of it.

Ads, through Google AdMob. BulkText shows a banner ad on its Broadcasts, Contacts and Templates screens, provided by Google AdMob. To show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK in BulkText sends Google:

  • your IP address, from which Google may estimate your approximate location;
  • interactions such as app launches and taps on ads;
  • diagnostic information about the performance of the app and the SDK;
  • device identifiers: the advertising ID, the app set ID, and, if applicable, other identifiers related to signed-in accounts on the device.

Google AdMob encrypts this data in transit. Google's use of it is described in "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services" (https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites) and in Google's privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy).

BulkText uses Google's consent tool, which contacts Google to learn which privacy rules apply where you are. When the consent tool says your consent is needed for ads, as Google requires in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, BulkText shows Google's consent form before it requests ads, and Settings → Ads → "Ad privacy choices" then lets you change your choice at any time. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings (usually under Privacy → Ads).

BulkText never gives your messages, phone numbers, contacts, recipients or broadcasts to Google or any other ad network, and never uses them for advertising. Ad requests contain no information from them.

Backup and export files, only where you choose. "Back up to a file" saves your contacts, groups, templates, do-not-message list and settings in a file, and exporting a broadcast saves its recipients and results as a CSV file. Each is written only to the place you pick, which may be your phone or a cloud drive you use. These files aren't encrypted and contain names and phone numbers, so keep them private.

Attachments you share, open in another app or save go only where you choose.

Sale or sharing. BulkText has no purchases or subscriptions, and we don't sell your data to anyone.

6. Children

BulkText is meant for adults (18 and over) and is not directed at children.

7. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

  • Data in BulkText stays on your phone until you delete it or uninstall BulkText.
  • In the app you can delete contacts, groups, templates, do-not-message entries, scheduled messages and messages that failed. This version can't delete conversations, broadcasts, or messages you sent or received, other than failed ones.
  • Unblocking a number, in BulkText or in your Phone app, removes it from the phone's block list and from BulkText's copy. Not spam removes a spam mark and, when the conversation's number is blocked, also takes the number off the block list.
  • Uninstalling BulkText, or clearing its storage in Android's app settings, deletes everything BulkText stores itself, including its copy of your messages and your ad consent choice. Your messages stay in Android's SMS storage, where another SMS app can show or delete them. Backup and export files you saved stay where you put them.
  • Uninstalling BulkText deletes its spam marks; numbers you blocked stay on the phone's block list.
  • BulkText's copy of your phone's contact names is deleted when contacts access is turned off.
  • Data Google receives for ads is kept under Google's policies. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings.
